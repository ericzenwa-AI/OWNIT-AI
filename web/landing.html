Why doing more past papers isn't working

Every A-level question sits on top of other things. Differentiation sits on indices. Indices sit on knowing what a power means. When a student gets a question wrong, the mark scheme says they got that topic wrong — so that's the topic they go back and revise.

But if the actual problem is two years further down, revising the topic changes nothing. They do it again, it still doesn't stick, and they conclude they're just bad at maths.

They're not. They can't see where it broke. Neither, from the outside, can you.

What examiners actually saw

Quite a few students expanded (x + h)³ as (x² + xh) + (xh)… because students did not collect like terms. AQA examiner report, 7357/1, June 2024

Every one of those students lost the marks on a differentiation question. But not one of them had a differentiation problem. They couldn't expand a bracket — something they were taught at fourteen.

What your student sees

How it works

  1. Send the question you're stuck on

    A photo of the page is best — typing maths out loses the powers and the fractions. Tell it what you tried, if you tried something.

  2. Answer a few short questions

    Usually about six. Multiple choice, and "I don't know" is always an option — it's more useful than a guess, and it isn't marked against you.

  3. Get the first thing to fix — and the route back

    Not a list of twelve weaknesses. One skill to start on, the reason it's that one and not something lower, and the route back up to the question they were stuck on. The first thing in the way — and it says so when there may be more behind it.

The obvious question

Couldn't you just ask a chatbot?

You can, and if you are good at prompting you will get something that looks a lot like this. Plenty of tutors already do. The difference is not the questions it asks — it is what happens to the answers.

When a model writes a diagnostic question, it also decides which option is correct. Nothing checks it. We measured that on this project: of the first 531 questions written, 55 had the wrong answer marked correct. Four in ten of the integration ones. Not one of them looked wrong.

The second one is worth two seconds of your time: u₁ + d is u₂, which the question gives you. Both were sitting in the bank ready to be asked, until a second model was made to work them out from scratch.

That is the worst thing a diagnostic can get wrong, because it is invisible. A student answers correctly, is recorded as having a gap, and you spend a session teaching something they already knew. The report looks completely normal.

Every question here has been solved blind by a second model that was never shown the stored answer. Where the two disagreed, a third decided. 77 were retired.

The rest of the difference is the map. What differentiation rests on is written down, not improvised fresh each time, so the same student gets the same answer on Tuesday as on Friday — and when it gets something wrong, you say so once and it stays corrected.

It won't do their homework

It never gives the student the answer to the question they sent. That's deliberate, and it's the whole point — an answer gets them through tonight, and they're stuck again next week on the same thing wearing a different hat.

They can show the result to their teacher without it looking like they cheated, because they didn't.
