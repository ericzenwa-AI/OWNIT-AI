Why doing more past papers isn't working

Every A-level question sits on top of other things. Differentiation sits on indices. Indices sit on knowing what a power means. When you get a question wrong, the mark scheme says you got that topic wrong — so that's the topic you go back and revise.

But if the actual problem is two years further down, revising the topic changes nothing. You do it again, it still doesn't stick, and you conclude you're just bad at maths.

You're not. You just can't see where it broke, because if you could see it, you wouldn't be stuck.

A real example

What examiners actually saw

June 2024, AQA Paper 1, question 10. Prove from first principles that the derivative of x³ is 3x². Here is what the examiners reported students doing:

Quite a few students expanded (x + h)³ as (x² + xh) + (xh)… because students did not collect like terms. AQA examiner report, 7357/1, June 2024

Every one of those students lost the marks on a differentiation question. But not one of them had a differentiation problem. They couldn't expand a bracket — something they were taught at fourteen.

How it works

  1. Send the question you're stuck on

    A photo of the page is best — typing maths out loses the powers and the fractions. Tell it what you tried, if you tried something.

  2. Answer a few short questions

    Usually about six. Multiple choice, and "I don't know" is always an option — it's more useful than a guess, and it isn't marked against you.

  3. Get one thing to fix

    Not a list of twelve weaknesses. One skill, the reason it's that one, and what it means you'll stop getting wrong once it's fixed.

It won't do your homework

It never gives you the answer to the question you sent. That's deliberate, and it's the whole point — an answer gets you through tonight, and you're stuck again next week on the same thing wearing a different hat.

You can show the result to your teacher or your tutor without it looking like you cheated, because you didn't.
